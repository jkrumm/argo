# Depot chart — concept

`/finance/depot` is where the owner reads a price chart next to his own trades. It replaces the habit of keeping the chart in TradingView
and marking buys and sells by hand: the market store, the depot and the hand-kept orders of the vault (`~/SourceRoot/finances`, contract
`docs/service.md` §Chart routes) are all served, so the page reads them in instead of asking him to draw them. Behind the finance gate like
its siblings; the market routes (bars, lists, search, series) answer while the vault is locked, the depot and drawing routes need it open.

## Question and decision

- **Question:** where is this instrument against what I paid, what I have waiting at the bank, and the levels I drew?
- **Decision:** wait, add to the position, or move an order. Nothing on the page places, changes or deletes an order at the bank:
  ING has no interface for orders, so the open orders are typed in here and closed here by hand.

The chart loads every stored bar of the instrument at once (the service answers up to 20,000; Bitcoin has about 5,500 daily bars since 2011) and
the **timeframes only move the view**: 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, 5Y and Max set the visible range, a drag scrolls and the wheel or a pinch zooms. Which button is
lit is read off the chart, so a zoom to something else lights none. It opens on the last year at the day interval; week and month open on 252 bars
(a year of trading days), so the candles are about as wide as at day and the distance between two bars does not jump when the interval changes. A
timeframe that would hold fewer than six bars at the interval (one month of week bars) is disabled. The page-bar range filter is gone from the chart; it
remains only for the index card. The market store has no intraday, so there is no minute or hour interval.

On a wide container the chart view fills the viewport under the page bar: the page body takes `--basalt-main-content-h` (never under 36 rem, then the page scrolls), the tabs what they need, the sidebar scrolls inside its column and the chart card hands what is left to the chart, so nothing scrolls at 1440 × 900 and the chart follows the window. A card is never under 30 rem, which keeps the Bitcoin page (its CBBI panel sits under the chart) at a readable chart and lets that page scroll. A narrow container (a phone) keeps a fixed height (26 rem, 44 rem from 480 px of card width); the other views take their own height.

## The live layer and the ING view

The owner set a VGLA order against yesterday's close, 3 % off ING's live quote (2026-11-26). ING Direkthandel is the price his orders meet, so the
page reads it live (finances `docs/service.md` §Chart routes: `market/quote`, `candles?today=1`; logic in `lib/depot-live.ts`).

- **Polling.** The quote route is asked for the charted instrument, the active tab's rows and the instruments an order can be for, batched, every
  60 s while any session of the batch is not closed and the tab is visible. All closed: once more at the next open, after 15 minutes at the latest
  (Direkthandel's pre-market starts before the regular open the quote names); a series is never asked again. A tab coming back asks at once.
- **The history is read once** (`today=1`, kept an hour). The running candle is a five-bar tail (`today=1&limit=5`) in the chart's interval and venue,
  on the same schedule: the service folds the day into a week or month, and Direkthandel's day exists only on the candles route. The chart takes the
  changed bar with `series.update`, so zoom and view stay; the candle is drawn like any other.
- **Header.** The live price leads, before the all-time high so the high is never read as the price: last price, change against the previous
  close, a status (`live`, `15 Min. verzögert`, `vorbörslich (venue)`, `nachbörslich (venue)`, `Börse geschlossen · Schluss 17:30`, `Kein
  Live-Kurs`) with the as-of time on hover or tap; a coin's hover says its day starts at 00:00 UTC. A euro line's price is Direkthandel's while the
  Xetra candle is 15 minutes behind: the header says `Kerze 15 Min. verzögert` beside it. A US stock's dollar chart reads its US listing.
- **Bid and ask** are thin dotted neutral lines, `Geld` under and `Brief` over, while the session is not closed; on a dollar chart at the latest ECB
  rate with the euro figure first.
- **Order lines** name the side they must meet and how far the order's price is from it: a buy limit against Brief, a sell limit against Geld, a stop
  against the last price (`Kauf Limit 4,21 € · Brief 4,23 € · −0,5 %`). A narrow chart breaks the label in two.
- **Order form.** A limit starts at the ask (buy) or the bid (sell), set once per instrument, side and type, never while he looks at it and never over a
  typed price; the spread stands under it.
- **Venue switch** under the chart, per instrument on this device: the exchange of the long history (default; Xetra, or the US listing) or ING
  Direkthandel's own stored days (`mic=ING-DH`), with a line saying how few there are. One venue per candle series, never mixed: Xetra closes at 17:30,
  Direkthandel at 23:00, and their closes differ. Trade markers stay as they are.
- **Sidebar and Board** show the live price and day change of the euro line. While a row's day is running the Board counts week, month, YTD, 1Y and
  the distance from the high again with the live price (from its newest 400 bars, read once) and marks the row with the status under its price.
- **No old quote reads as live.** When a refresh fails or is paused, the price stays with its time under `Kein Live-Kurs`: no bid or ask, no order distance, no prefilled limit.
- **Not pushed yet:** `market/stream` (server-sent events) would need a relay route of its own; polling is what was asked.

## Index entries and their ETFs

An index cannot be held, but the owner follows the long index series and buys the young ETF that tracks it (owner feedback 2026-11-26: an order on the ETF could not be recorded from the index entry). The tracking ETFs of a series are the watchlist rows whose `index` is its id (`lib/depot-tracking.ts`).

- **Details** shows a position strip per tracking ETF, their trades and their open orders (an Instrument column when there are several) and the all-time statistics when there is exactly one. "Add order" opens the form with an ETF select, the held one (else the first) preselected; the order is kept on the ETF's ISIN.
- **Chart** draws those open orders and trade markers at the equivalent index level: the ETF price × (index close ÷ ETF close in euros), on the latest day both have a close, the index close in the chart's currency. A line therefore sits as far from the index close as the limit is from the ETF close. The label keeps the ETF's own price ("Kauf Limit 4,21 €", with its side and distance, plus the ETF's name when there are several). A marker sits at that level instead of above or below its bar; the same one factor is used for old trades, so a price index and an accumulating fund drift apart by the dividends over years.

## Currency

A chart that is natively in dollars (a US stock on its US listing, a dollar index, Bitcoin) has a **USD / EUR** switch under it, remembered per instrument on this device (`depot-view`). Every chart opens in dollars.

- **EUR** converts each day bar at the ECB `eur-usd` value of its day (the latest earlier day on a gap; a bar before the first rate is left out). Week and month candles are built in the page from the converted days, so each open and close carries its own day's rate (`lib/depot-currency.ts`). The axis, readout, header, all-time high and labels follow; the sidebar and the Board stay in the instrument's currency.
- An order is a euro price, so a US stock's line on the euro chart sits at the typed limit and its average cost is the euro cost.
- **Drawings stay stored in the instrument's own currency**: a euro view shows them at the latest rate and divides by it on save, so no stored document changes meaning and the toggle never moves a line.
- The order form takes the price in euros (ING trades in euros) and, for a dollar chart, shows the dollar equivalent at the latest rate.

## Listings and currency

Bitcoin is read from its dollar listing (`BTC-USD`; the euro listing is not offered), and the USD / EUR switch converts it like any dollar chart. An instrument the watchlist says is charted as another listing (a Bitcoin ETP, `instruments` of the `BTC-USD` row) has no row of its own: the row is held when any of its instruments is, its trades come from
`depot/trades?isin=BTC-USD`, and the position strip sums them. A US stock with a stored US listing (`us_mic`) is read on that listing, in dollars unless switched to euros.
Trade markers sit on the candle of the booking day in either case; the trade card adds the dollar price (`price_usd`, the ECB rate of the booking day).
The position strip, the trades table's amounts and orders stay in euros. The table adds a dollar price column where a trade has one.

An Xetra ETF with a tracked index (`index` of its watchlist row, set with `fin market watch add --index`) is asked for with `splice=index`: the answer opens with the index's levels as flat,
flagged bars (`source: "index"`, no turnover), converted with the ECB rate and scaled by one factor to meet the ETF's first close. A week or month bar is an index bar when every day in it is one.

## Comparison

"Compare with…" is a menu in the chart's bottom toolbar, beside Log and Volume; it names a stored index (`?cmp=`) and **is off by default**. The chosen index is drawn on the candles' own price scale, which switches to the library's percentage mode: each series is rebased to its first visible value, so the rebase follows the timeframe buttons, zoom and scroll with no code of ours, and 6M and 1Y read differently. There is no second area. Percent mode and the log scale exclude each other, so Log is disabled while comparing (its setting returns when the comparison is removed with the chip's x). The index is resampled to the chart's bar times (the last level on or before a bar, at most ten days old) so week and month charts keep their spacing. The chip measures the instrument over the span the index line covers (a young fund's spliced stretch counts); the spliced index bars and the fund's own bars are one candlestick series, so the library's rebase has one base (the first visible bar of the whole history) and the fund's candles continue from where the index ended instead of starting again at 0 % on their first bar.

The comparison exists because the index behind a young fund is not free: FTSE Global All Cap levels need an LSEG licence, so an ETF like VGLA (launched 2026-08) is read against MSCI ACWI IMI, which has the same size segments and about 63 % US weight, from 1998 on. The fund line is its close, so a distributing fund's payouts are not in it; the index line is a net return. The index is not the fund's own benchmark, so the gap is not tracking error. The spliced history in the chart is the fund's _tracked_ index (FTSE, price level); the comparison is the MSCI stand-in: two different questions. A comparison with another instrument is not offered; the URL field names the five stored indices.

## Numbers

German everywhere on the page (`lib/depot-number.ts`): a dot groups thousands, a comma opens the decimals, a minus is the real sign (−). A price has as many decimals as its size asks: none from 1.000 (`95.433`), two from 10 (`182,40`), three from 1 (`4,812`), four below. The price axis, the crosshair label, the readout, the header, the line labels (trailing zeros dropped: `182 €`), the drawings' own labels, the trade cards, the tables and the position strip use the same rules; the turnover axis reads `12 Mio.`. A percent has one decimal: `−18,3 %`, `+5,1 %`. Dates are `24.11.2026`.

## Header facts

The card header carries the **all-time high**: its value in the chart's currency, the day it was reached and, beside it, the distance from it of the live price (`−6,2 % now vs. high`) or, with the market closed, of the last close (`last close vs. high`). The high is the greatest daily high of the instrument's own bars, the running day included (a spliced index does not count); on week and month charts the day bars are asked for separately, so the day is exact. It is read from the whole stored history, whatever the visible range.

## Drawings

- The package paints a drawing in its own blue while it is placed. `setDefaultStyleOverride` gives every tool the chosen colour before the first click, so the
  preview and the placed drawing look the same.
- **One toolbar.** The pointer, horizontal line, vertical line and price range are in the chart card's header; each has an icon and a tooltip naming it and what it marks ("Horizontal line: marks a price", "Vertical line: marks a day"). The three colours sit beside them: they colour the next line and, while the pointer is on and one line is selected, that line. A tool is used once: the pointer comes back after every drawing.
- **Line editor.** Selecting one line (a click on it, or placing it) opens a small panel over the top of the chart with the colours, a label field (Enter or leaving the field applies, Escape puts the line's own back), for a horizontal line its price, and delete. The price is typed German: `150`, `150,5`, `95.000` and `95.000,5` all read; a dot that is followed by exactly three digits is a thousands separator, any other dot a decimal point. Enter or leaving the field moves the line and writes the price back with grouping; Escape restores it; text that is no price reverts. The move is saved like any edit.
- One JSON document per instrument, `{ v: 1, drawings: [...], foreign: [...], roles: { <id>: 'accent' | 'buy' | 'sell' } }`; the server never reads it. A document without `roles` (the first version) reads as all accent.
- Saved about 500 ms after the last change (flushed on instrument switch, unmount and when the tab hides); a failed save is
  reported and retried with the next change while the instrument stays open. Leaving it (or a vault lock that
  drops the page to the unlock screen) with a save pending and failing loses that last change: the document is not kept
  outside the page.
- An entry the package cannot read after an upgrade (no id back from `importJSON`) moves to `foreign`, is offered to the package again at
  every load and written back on every save: never dropped.
- Delete and Escape work on the chart and ignore the keys while a form field has focus.

## Decisions

- **Direct `lightweight-charts`, outside basalt-ui's chart primitives.** basalt-ui's charts have no candlesticks, no markers bound to a bar and no
  drawing tools. Owner decision 2026-11-22, recorded in `DESIGN.md` §App deviations. `basalt/chart-engine-boundary` polices only `@visx` and
  `@tanstack/charts`, so no lint exception exists or is needed. Both libraries are imported by `components/depot-chart.tsx` and nothing else.
- **The whole history is loaded, the timeframes are a view** (owner feedback 2026-11-24): a range filter that decided what was loaded hid the data the owner wanted to scroll to. The cost is one answer of at most a few hundred kilobytes per instrument; paging older bars on scroll was left out because no stored history comes near the 20,000-bar cap.
- **Log scale and turnover are remembered per instrument, on this device**, in a `createPersistedState` map keyed by ISIN (`lib/view-prefs.ts`) and not as store fields: a store declares its fields once, and the instruments are open-ended. Two buttons bind it, no filter or tab does (`basalt:depot-view` in localStorage, no vault data), with defaults by kind: a coin opens logarithmic; a coin, an index or an instrument with no turnover opens without the turnover pane.
- **The spliced history is lighter candles, not a line** (owner feedback 2026-11-25, replacing the line decision of 2026-11-24): the market store holds real daily open, high, low and close of the tracked FTSE index from 2013-03-01, so the bars are real. It is one candlestick series with the ETF's own bars (owner feedback 2026-11-26: a separate series is rebased to 0 % at its first bar in the percent scale, so the ETF's candles jumped back at the launch), the index part carrying per-bar colours at 65 % opacity; the price lines, markers and drawings sit on that one series. A series with closes only (`derived_from_close`) stays a line.
- **The chart fills the height; the picks keep the view; the Board lists the whole tab** (owner feedback 2026-11-25): see "Layout" above, and `depot-page.md` for the view and the Board.
- **Right margin, line labels, Beschriftung** (owner feedback 2026-11-26): every view (load, interval, timeframe button, instrument change) leaves about 5 % of the visible width empty right of the newest bar (the empty bars derive from the visible bar count, `rightMarginBars`). The order and cost titles are not the library's price-line titles (those sit at the right edge over the newest candles and cannot move): the lines carry no title and a series primitive writes it at the left edge, above the line, with a halo in the card colour. The toolbar toggle **Beschriftung** (the owner's word, kept German) hides the text labels of horizontal and vertical lines (own drawings, open orders, average cost) and is remembered for every instrument on this device (`depot-line-labels`); lines and axis prices stay, a hidden drawing label keeps its text in the document and is only painted clear.
